    Section 8.1 Laws of Sines and Cosines

    In this section, we will learn how to use the laws of sines and cosines to find missing sides and angles in non-right triangles. Then we can use these laws to solve applications.

    Subsection Definitions

    Definition 8.1.1. Law of Sines.

    The law of sines says that the ratios of sines of angles in a triangle and the opposite sides are all constant. That’s a bit of a mouthful, maybe a math-ful will help. Consider the triangle in Figure [provisional cross-reference: def-law-of-sines]. The law of sines says that...
    \begin{equation*} \frac{\sin(A)}{a} = \frac{\sin(B)}{b} = \frac{\sin(C)}{c} \end{equation*}
    or alternately,
    \begin{equation*} \frac{a}{\sin(A)} = \frac{b}{\sin(B)} = \frac{c}{\sin(C)} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 8.1.2.

    When using the law of sines, you almost never need all three parts of the equation at the same time, so you will end up using one of these at a time:
    \begin{align*} \frac{\sin(A)}{a} \amp= \frac{\sin(B)}{b}\\ \frac{\sin(B)}{b} \amp= \frac{\sin(C)}{c}\\ \frac{\sin(C)}{c} \amp= \frac{\sin(A)}{a} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 8.1.3. Law of Cosines.

    The law of cosines is really the natural extension of the Pythagorean Theorem for triangles without a right angle. Consider the triangle in Figure [provisional cross-reference: def-law-of-cosines]. The law of cosines says that...
    \begin{equation*} a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos(A) \end{equation*}
    or
    \begin{equation*} b^2 = a^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos(B) \end{equation*}
    or
    \begin{equation*} c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2bc\cos(C) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 8.1.4.

    Unlike the Pythagorean Theorem, you don’t have to put the longest length alone on one side. It can be inputted for \(a\text{,}\) \(b\text{,}\) or \(c\text{.}\)
