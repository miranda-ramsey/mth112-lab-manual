    Section 8.2 Polar Coordinates

    In this section, we will learn a completely different way of organizing the coordinate plane: polar coordinates, where a polar point, \((\rho,\theta)\text{,}\) has a radius \(\rho\) from the origin and the angle from standard position, \(\theta\text{.}\) We will practice plotting points using polar coordinates, converting between polar coordinates and rectangular coordinates, transforming equations between polar and rectangular forms, and graphing polar equations.
    🔗

    Subsection Textbook Reference

    This relates to content in §10.3 and §10.4 of Algebra and Trigonometry 2e.

    🔗

    Subsection Definitions

    Definition 8.2.7. Polar Function.

    A polar function is usually given in the form \(\rho = f(\theta)\) where \(\theta\) is the angle measured from the standard position along the positive \(x\)-axis, and \(\rho\) is the radius measured from the origin.
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 8.2.8. Conversion formulas.

    • To convert from rectangular form to polar form, use these two identities.
      \begin{gather*} r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \text{ or } r^2 = x^2 + y^2\\ \tan(\theta = \frac{y}{x} \end{gather*}
      🔗
    • To convert from polar form to rectangular form, use these two identities.
      \begin{gather*} x = r\,\cos(\theta)\\ y = r\,\sin(\theta) \end{gather*}
      🔗
